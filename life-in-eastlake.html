City close, country quiet

Why Locals Love Living in Eastlake

When my family moved here in 1987, Eastlake was brand new: a lake, a Beach Club and rolling hills where most of today's neighborhoods now stand. Nearly 40 years later, the things that drew us here are still what people love most.

A lake in the middle of town

Anyone can enjoy the walking path around the Eastlake lake, and for Eastlake I residents in the Hills and Shores, the paddleboats and Beach Club lagoon add a resort feel you don't expect in South County.

Clubs, pools and courts close to home

Eastlake has three separate HOAs, and each has its own clubhouses, pools and parks just for its residents: the Beach Club in Eastlake I, the Tennis & Swim Club in Eastlake II (the Greens) and the Creekside and Woods clubhouses in Eastlake III.

Open space on every side

Greenbelts, the golf course, Mountain Hawk Park and the Otay Lakes keep Eastlake feeling open, with hiking and biking minutes from your front door.

Olympic-level neighbors

The Elite Athlete Training Center, the former U.S. Olympic Training Center, is right next door, and Eastlake Little League brought home the 2013 U.S. championship.

Getting around

Close to the City, Easy to Come Home To

State Route 125, the South Bay Expressway, runs right through Eastlake, linking the neighborhoods north toward East County and the rest of San Diego and south toward Otay Mesa and the border crossing. East H Street, Otay Lakes Road, Proctor Valley Road, Eastlake Parkway, Hunte Parkway and Olympic Parkway carry most local trips.

Everyday errands stay close: the Eastlake Village Center, Village Marketplace and Village Walk cover groceries, dining and shopping, and Otay Ranch Town Center is just south.

Questions, answered

Frequently Asked Questions

What is there to do in Eastlake, Chula Vista?

Each of Eastlake's three HOAs has its own amenities for its residents. Eastlake I (Hills and Shores) has the Beach Club, the lagoon and paddleboats, and the walking path around the Eastlake lake is open to everyone. Eastlake II (Greens) has the Tennis & Swim Club, Dolphin Beach and Augusta Park. Eastlake III (Trails, Trails North, The Woods and Vistas) has the Creekside and Woods clubhouses and Eastlake Pond. Open to everyone nearby are the Enagic Golf Club, Eastlake Little League, Mountain Hawk Park, the Otay Lakes, Mother Miguel Mountain, the Elite Athlete Training Center, Sesame Place San Diego and the North Island Credit Union Amphitheatre.

Is Eastlake close to the Otay Lakes?

Yes. The Lower Otay Reservoir sits on Eastlake's eastern edge, near Eastlake Vistas, The Woods and Mountain Hawk Park, with fishing, boating, picnics and trails.

Where do Eastlake residents shop?

Most everyday shopping is at the Eastlake Village Center, Eastlake Village Marketplace and Village Walk at Eastlake. Otay Ranch Town Center, an outdoor mall with dining and a movie theater, is just south.

Do Eastlake HOAs include amenities?

Yes. Each of Eastlake's three HOAs has its own amenities for residents, such as the Beach Club in Eastlake I, the Tennis & Swim Club in Eastlake II and the Creekside and Woods clubhouses in Eastlake III. See the Eastlake HOAs and amenities guide on this site to compare them.
