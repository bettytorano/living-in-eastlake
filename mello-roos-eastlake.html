Mello-Roos in Eastlake: who still pays, what it pays for, and how to check any home

I paid Mello-Roos on my Eastlake Hills home for 25 years, and then it ended. That's true for some Eastlake homes and not for others. Here's how it really works in Eastlake, and how to look up any address in about ten minutes.

By Betty Torano, REALTOR® · Eastlake resident since 1987 · Last reviewed October 8, 2026

The short version

It's a special tax

Mello-Roos appears on your county property tax bill, on top of the regular 1% tax and separate from HOA dues.

Homes can have more than one

School, city and maintenance districts can all appear, depending on the parcel.

Some end, some don't

Bond-funded taxes can end. City open space charges are designed to be permanent.

Never assume

Check the tax bill for the specific parcel, not the neighborhood name.

1
The basics

What Mello-Roos is, and why Eastlake has it

Mello-Roos comes from California's Mello-Roos Community Facilities Act of 1982. After Proposition 13 capped property taxes in 1978, cities and school districts needed another way to pay for schools, roads, parks and infrastructure in brand-new communities. The answer was the Community Facilities District, or CFD: a defined area where property owners pay a special tax that funds those improvements.

Eastlake was one of South County's first big master-planned communities, built in phases starting in the 1980s. The schools, parkways and landscaped open space that make Eastlake feel like Eastlake were largely paid for this way.

“When my family moved in during 1987, we paid Mello-Roos in addition to our regular property taxes and HOA dues. Nobody thought twice about it. It was simply the cost of a brand-new community.”Betty

Know the difference

Mello-Roos, HOA dues and property tax are three different things

Buyers often lump these together. They're billed differently, pay for different things, and end (or don't) on different schedules.

Eastlake districts

The special tax and assessment districts that touch Eastlake

These are the districts I see most often on Eastlake tax bills. Which ones apply depends on the exact parcel, so treat this as a map of what to look for, not a list of what you'll pay.

Mello-Roos · school

Sweetwater Union High School District CFD No. 1 (Eastlake)

Sweetwater has used Mello-Roos districts since 1986 to help pay for school facilities, and its first district, CFD No. 1, is named Eastlake. Rates are set every year. For scale, the 2015–16 levy was $679.47 per dwelling unit; look up today's figure for your parcel.

Mello-Roos · maintenance

City of Chula Vista CFD 07M (Eastlake III, Woods & Vistas)

The “M” stands for maintenance. CFD 07M funds ongoing upkeep of public landscaping and open space in the Woods and Vistas area, rather than construction.

Assessment · not Mello-Roos

Open Space District 101 (Eastlake Maintenance District #1)

Formed in 1986 to maintain open space in Eastlake Hills, Shores, the Village Center, Business Center and northern Greens: parkway landscaping, irrigation, lighting and open space lots. Five zones, so amounts vary. Increases are limited by Proposition 218.

Varies

Other districts that may appear

Chula Vista Elementary School District CFDs in newer areas, other city districts, and county or water charges. The tax bill is the only reliable list.

4
Why it ends

“My Mello-Roos ended.” Why that can be true and you still see special charges

Bond-funded Mello-Roos

Repays construction bonds. Once the bonds are paid off and the district's obligations are met, the tax can stop. That's what happened with our Eastlake Hills home after 25 years. Newer neighborhoods were built later, so their districts generally have more years left.

Maintenance districts

The City of Chula Vista set up its open space districts “to ensure financing for perpetual maintenance of public open space areas,” so they're designed to stay. Annual increases are capped at the lower of the San Diego CPI and California's per-capita income index unless owners vote for more.

A real example: my own Eastlake Hills tax bill

When I pulled up my current bill, there were no Mello-Roos (CFD) lines left. It still lists several other fixed charges from the city, county and water agencies, such as EASTLK MAINT #1 ZN A for $18.52 (Eastlake Maintenance District #1, Zone A, the city's open space maintenance assessment) and CWA WTR AVAILABILITY, a water availability charge.

None of those are Mello-Roos. They're normal charges that most San Diego County homes see, and they're part of why the parkways still look the way they do.

5
Check any home

How to check any Eastlake home in about ten minutes

  1. 1

    Pull up the property tax bill

    Go to the San Diego County Treasurer-Tax Collector at sdttc.com, search by address or parcel number (APN), and open the current secured tax bill.

  2. 2

    Find the special tax lines

    Look past the 1% base tax and voter-approved bonds to the fixed charges and special assessments. Names are abbreviated, for example “SUHSD CFD,” “Special Tax,” “EASTLK MAINT #1 ZN A” or “CWA WTR AVAILABILITY,” and each lists the agency and a phone number. Read each line's name rather than relying on the section heading.

  3. 3

    Ask each agency two questions

    “What is the current annual amount for this parcel?” and “When is this tax scheduled to end, if ever?”

  4. 4

    For the Sweetwater school district

    Use the district's special tax site at specialdistricttransparency.com/suhsd.

  5. 5

    If you're buying, get the Notice of Special Tax

    California Civil Code §1102.6b requires sellers to make a good-faith effort to get this notice from each agency that levies a Mello-Roos tax and give it to the buyer. Your preliminary title report lists these districts too.

What it means for you

Buying or selling in Eastlake

If you're buying

  • Compare total monthly cost, not price. Add special taxes ÷ 12, HOA dues (master plus any sub-association) and base property tax. Two homes at the same price can be hundreds of dollars a month apart.
  • Your lender will count it. Special taxes are part of your tax bill, so they're in your payment and loan qualification.
  • Ask about the end date. A tax ending in a few years matters when you compare neighborhoods.

If you're selling

  • If your Mello-Roos has ended, say so. Lower carrying costs are a real selling point.
  • Gather the notices early. Requesting the Notice of Special Tax before you list keeps escrow on schedule.
  • Price with the full picture. I account for special taxes when I prepare your home value.

This guide is general information to help you ask the right questions. It is not tax, legal or financial advice. Confirm amounts and end dates for a specific property with the agency that levies each tax, and talk to a tax professional about your situation.

Questions, answered

Frequently asked questions

Does Eastlake have Mello-Roos?

Yes. Mello-Roos taxes come from Community Facilities Districts (CFDs). In Eastlake these include Sweetwater Union High School District's CFD No. 1 (Eastlake), which funds school facilities, and the City of Chula Vista's CFD 07M, a maintenance district for the Woods and Vistas. Some original neighborhoods have paid off their school Mello-Roos. Separately, the city's Open Space District 101 (Eastlake Maintenance District #1) is an assessment district, not Mello-Roos, even though the county lists it with the CFD charges. Which ones apply depends on the exact parcel.

Do older Eastlake neighborhoods still pay Mello-Roos?

Some original neighborhoods have paid off their school Mello-Roos. My Eastlake Hills home paid it for 25 years and then it ended. You may still see a small city open space maintenance assessment on the bill. That is not Mello-Roos, and it is designed to continue permanently.

What is EASTLK MAINT #1 on my Eastlake property tax bill?

It is the City of Chula Vista's Eastlake Maintenance District #1 (Open Space District 101), which pays for open space and parkway landscaping, irrigation and lighting in Eastlake Hills, Eastlake Shores, the Village Center, the Business Center and the northern portion of Eastlake Greens. The "ZN" letter is your zone. The county may list it with the CFD charges, but it is a city maintenance assessment, not a Mello-Roos tax.

How do I find out if a home has Mello-Roos?

Look up the property tax bill at sdttc.com, find the special tax and assessment lines, and call the agency listed for each one to ask the current amount and end date. Buyers should also ask for the Notice of Special Tax.

Is Mello-Roos the same as HOA dues?

No. Mello-Roos is a special tax on your county property tax bill. HOA dues are billed separately by your association.

Will Mello-Roos increase every year?

It can. Many districts allow a set annual increase, and City of Chula Vista open space assessment increases are capped at the lower of two published inflation measures unless property owners approve more. Ask the agency for the formula for your district.

Do sellers have to disclose Mello-Roos?

Yes. California Civil Code §1102.6b requires sellers to make a good-faith effort to get the Notice of Special Tax from each agency that levies a Mello-Roos tax on the home and give it to the buyer.
