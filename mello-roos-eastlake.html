Mello-Roos in Eastlake: who still pays, what it pays for, and how to check any home

I paid Mello-Roos on my Eastlake Hills home for 25 years, and then it ended. That's true for some Eastlake homes and not for others. Here's how it really works in Eastlake, and how to look up any address in about ten minutes.

By Betty Torano, REALTOR® · Eastlake resident since 1987 · Last reviewed October 8, 2026

The short version

  • Mello-Roos is a special tax that appears on your county property tax bill, on top of the regular 1% property tax and separate from HOA dues.
  • Eastlake homes can carry more than one district. Sweetwater Union High School District's CFD No. 1 is named "Eastlake," the city has a maintenance CFD for the Woods and Vistas, and the city also charges open space maintenance assessments (which are not Mello-Roos) in other Eastlake neighborhoods.
  • Some charges end and some don't. Taxes that repay construction bonds can end once the bonds are paid. City open space districts were set up to pay for maintenance permanently.
  • Never assume. Check the property tax bill for the specific parcel and call the agency listed next to each special tax line.

What Mello-Roos is (and why Eastlake has it)

Mello-Roos comes from California's Mello-Roos Community Facilities Act of 1982. After Proposition 13 capped property taxes in 1978, cities and school districts needed another way to pay for schools, roads, parks and infrastructure in brand-new communities. The answer was the Community Facilities District, or CFD: a defined area where property owners pay a special tax that funds those improvements.

Eastlake was one of South County's first big master-planned communities, built in phases starting in the 1980s. The schools, parkways and landscaped open space that make Eastlake feel like Eastlake were largely paid for this way. When my family moved in during 1987, we paid Mello-Roos in addition to our regular property taxes and HOA dues. Nobody thought twice about it, because it was simply the cost of a brand-new community.

Mello-Roos, HOA dues and property tax are three different things

Buyers often lump these together. They're billed differently, they pay for different things, and they end (or don't) on different schedules.

The special tax and assessment districts that touch Eastlake

These are the districts I see most often on Eastlake tax bills. Which ones apply depends on the exact parcel, so treat this as a map of what to look for, not a list of what you'll pay.

Sweetwater Union High School District: CFD No. 1 (Eastlake)

Sweetwater Union High School District has used Mello-Roos districts since 1986 to help pay for school facilities in new communities, and its very first district, CFD No. 1, is named Eastlake. The district's board sets the special tax rates every year. To give you a sense of scale, the district's levy resolution for the 2015–16 tax year set CFD No. 1 at $679.47 per dwelling unit. Today's amount will be different, so look up the current figure for your parcel.

City of Chula Vista: Open Space District 101 (Eastlake Maintenance District #1), an assessment, not Mello-Roos

The city formed this district in 1986 to maintain the natural and landscaped open space within Eastlake Hills, Eastlake Shores, the Village Center, the Business Center and the northern portion of Eastlake Greens. It pays for things like parkway landscaping and irrigation, supplemental street lighting, open space lots and a share of the Telegraph Canyon Channel. It's divided into five zones, so the amount varies by location. Technically this is an assessment district, not a Mello-Roos CFD: the city levies it as an annual assessment, and increases are limited by Proposition 218. It still shows up in the special charges section of your tax bill.

City of Chula Vista: CFD 07M (Eastlake III, Woods & Vistas)

The "M" stands for maintenance. CFD 07M is a Mello-Roos district that funds ongoing maintenance of public landscaping and open space for the Woods and Vistas area of Eastlake III. It's a different kind of Mello-Roos from the school bond taxes, because it pays for upkeep rather than construction.

Other districts that may appear

Chula Vista Elementary School District also uses community facilities districts for school facilities in newer areas of eastern Chula Vista. Depending on when and where a home was built, you may see an elementary school district line, other city districts, or water and sewer charges. The tax bill is the only reliable list.

"My Mello-Roos ended." Why that can be true and you still see special charges

This is the part that confuses people most, and it's why I tell everyone to read the actual bill.

Bond-funded Mello-Roos taxes repay construction bonds. Once the bonds are paid off and the district's obligations are met, the tax can stop. That's what happened with our Eastlake Hills home after 25 years. Newer Eastlake neighborhoods were built later, so their districts were formed later and generally have more years left.

Maintenance districts work differently. The City of Chula Vista says its open space districts were established "to ensure financing for perpetual maintenance of public open space areas." In other words, those charges are designed to stay. The upside is that annual increases are limited: the city caps them at the lower of two published inflation measures, the San Diego CPI and California's per-capita personal income index, unless property owners vote for more.

So an Eastlake Hills home today might have no school Mello-Roos at all but still show an open space assessment. That's normal, and it's part of why the parkways still look the way they do.

A real example: my own Eastlake Hills tax bill

When I pulled up my current bill, there were no Mello-Roos (CFD) lines left. The only special line was EASTLK MAINT #1 ZN A for $18.52. That's Eastlake Maintenance District #1 (Open Space District 101), Zone A, which is the city's open space maintenance assessment.

How to check any Eastlake home in about ten minutes

  1. Pull up the property tax bill. Go to the San Diego County Treasurer-Tax Collector at sdttc.com and search by address or parcel number (APN). Open the current secured tax bill.
  2. Find the special tax lines. Look past the 1% base tax and voter-approved bonds to the fixed charges and special assessments. Names are often abbreviated, for example "SUHSD CFD," "Special Tax" or "EASTLK MAINT #1 ZN A," and each line lists the agency and a phone number. The county may group open space maintenance charges with the CFD charges, so read each line's name rather than relying on the section heading.
  3. Ask each agency two questions: "What is the current annual amount for this parcel?" and "When is this tax scheduled to end, if ever?"
  4. For the Sweetwater school district, use the district's special tax information site at specialdistricttransparency.com/suhsd, which the district points property owners to for CFD details.
  5. If you're buying, ask for the Notice of Special Tax. California Civil Code §1102.6b requires sellers to make a good-faith effort to get this notice from each agency that levies a Mello-Roos tax on the home and give it to the buyer. Your preliminary title report will list these districts too.

Or send me the address. I'm happy to look it up with you.

What it means if you're buying in Eastlake

  • Compare total monthly cost, not price. Add the annual special taxes divided by 12, the HOA dues (master plus any sub-association) and the base property tax. Two homes at the same price can be hundreds of dollars a month apart.
  • Your lender will count it. Special taxes are part of your property tax bill, so they're usually included in your monthly payment and your loan qualification.
  • Ask about the end date. A tax ending in a few years is worth knowing about when you compare neighborhoods.

What it means if you're selling in Eastlake

  • If your Mello-Roos has ended, say so. Lower carrying costs are a real selling point, especially compared with newer communities nearby.
  • Gather the notices early. Requesting the Notice of Special Tax from each agency before you list keeps escrow on schedule.
  • Price with the full picture. Buyers compare monthly costs, so I account for special taxes when I prepare your home value.

This guide is general information to help you ask the right questions. It is not tax, legal or financial advice. Confirm amounts and end dates for a specific property with the agency that levies each tax, and talk to a tax professional about your situation.

Questions, answered

Frequently asked questions

Does Eastlake have Mello-Roos?

Yes. Mello-Roos taxes come from Community Facilities Districts (CFDs). In Eastlake these include Sweetwater Union High School District's CFD No. 1 (Eastlake), which funds school facilities, and the City of Chula Vista's CFD 07M, a maintenance district for the Woods and Vistas. Some original neighborhoods have paid off their school Mello-Roos. Separately, the city's Open Space District 101 (Eastlake Maintenance District #1) is an assessment district, not Mello-Roos, even though the county lists it with the CFD charges. Which ones apply depends on the exact parcel.

Do older Eastlake neighborhoods still pay Mello-Roos?

Some original neighborhoods have paid off their school Mello-Roos. My Eastlake Hills home paid it for 25 years and then it ended. You may still see a small city open space maintenance assessment on the bill. That is not Mello-Roos, and it is designed to continue permanently.

What is EASTLK MAINT #1 on my Eastlake property tax bill?

It is the City of Chula Vista's Eastlake Maintenance District #1 (Open Space District 101), which pays for open space and parkway landscaping, irrigation and lighting in Eastlake Hills, Eastlake Shores, the Village Center, the Business Center and the northern portion of Eastlake Greens. The "ZN" letter is your zone. The county may list it with the CFD charges, but it is a city maintenance assessment, not a Mello-Roos tax.

How do I find out if a home has Mello-Roos?

Look up the property tax bill at sdttc.com, find the special tax and assessment lines, and call the agency listed for each one to ask the current amount and end date. Buyers should also ask for the Notice of Special Tax.

Is Mello-Roos the same as HOA dues?

No. Mello-Roos is a special tax on your county property tax bill. HOA dues are billed separately by your association.

Will Mello-Roos increase every year?

It can. Many districts allow a set annual increase, and City of Chula Vista open space assessment increases are capped at the lower of two published inflation measures unless property owners approve more. Ask the agency for the formula for your district.

Do sellers have to disclose Mello-Roos?

Yes. California Civil Code §1102.6b requires sellers to make a good-faith effort to get the Notice of Special Tax from each agency that levies a Mello-Roos tax on the home and give it to the buyer.
