What Are the Biggest Mistakes Homeowners Make When Selling in Eastlake, Chula Vista?

By Betty Torano, REALTOR® · Eastlake resident since 1987 · August 23, 2026

If you're getting ready to sell your home in Eastlake, Chula Vista, there are a few mistakes that can make the process harder than it needs to be.

The biggest ones I see are overpricing the home, failing to properly stage and prepare it before professional photography, and listing without a real marketing strategy.

I've been an Eastlake resident since 1987, so this isn't simply an area where I work. It's the community I've called home for decades. Selling a home here isn't just about putting it on the MLS and waiting for buyers. How you price, prepare, stage, photograph and market your home from the beginning matters, and all of those pieces need to work together.

1. Overpricing your Eastlake home

This is one of the biggest mistakes sellers make. I understand why it happens. You've put years into your home. Maybe you remodeled the kitchen, upgraded the backyard, replaced flooring or watched a nearby home sell for a number that caught your attention. Naturally, you want to get as much as possible, and you should.

But there's a difference between trying to get top dollar and starting at a price the current market doesn't support. If you overprice your home from the beginning, it can be difficult to recapture the buyers you lost.

When a new listing hits the market, buyers who have been waiting for a home in Eastlake may see it almost immediately through their agents and listing alerts. That initial attention is valuable. If those buyers decide the home is overpriced, many will simply move on. Then the seller reduces the price, and now you're trying to convince buyers who already passed on the property to look again.

Once you start reducing the price, you can find yourself chasing the market instead of having buyers chasing your home.

How should you price a home in Eastlake?

Pricing should be based on what's actually happening around your home, not simply the number you hope to receive. I look at recent comparable sales, current competing listings, property condition, location within the community, features and upgrades, current buyer demand and how long similar homes are taking to sell.

Eastlake isn't one identical housing market. Homes differ based on neighborhood, location, lot, view, upgrades, HOA or Mello-Roos considerations and property type. The goal isn't to price your home cheaply. It's to position it correctly so buyers see the value from the beginning.

2. Not staging and preparing your home before the photoshoot

Professional photography is important, but before the photographer ever walks through the door, the home needs to be ready to photograph. A photographer can only capture what's in front of the camera. If rooms are cluttered, furniture overwhelms the space, countertops are covered or the exterior hasn't been prepared, photography alone can't solve that.

Staging doesn't necessarily mean emptying the house and bringing in all-new furniture. Sometimes it's about working with what you already have:

  • Decluttering rooms
  • Removing distracting personal items
  • Clearing kitchen and bathroom countertops
  • Rearranging or removing furniture
  • Making rooms feel more open
  • Improving curb appeal
  • Cleaning and preparing outdoor spaces
  • Making sure the home feels clean, bright and inviting

We want buyers looking at the home, not everything inside it.

3. Treating professional photography like an afterthought

I remember one listing appointment where the seller asked if I was going to take pictures of the house that day with my phone. My answer was simple: "Absolutely not. I hire a professional photographer."

You only have one chance to make a great first impression, especially online. Before many buyers ever walk through your front door, they've already walked through your home on their screen. The sequence matters: prepare the home, stage the home, professionally photograph the home, then market it.

4. Thinking the MLS is the entire marketing strategy

The MLS is important, but marketing shouldn't stop there. When I list a home, my approach can include digital marketing, social media advertising, open houses, professional photography and direct neighborhood outreach such as door knocking.

You don't always know where the right buyer will come from. Maybe someone sees the property online, an open house brings in a buyer, or a social media ad reaches someone who wasn't actively searching that day. Getting more people to see a home only helps if they like what they see, which takes us right back to preparation.

5. Ignoring the power of the neighborhood

Sometimes one of your best marketing opportunities is right next door. When appropriate, I door knock around a listing and let neighbors know about the home, because neighbors know people. They may have friends, relatives or coworkers who already love Eastlake and would welcome the chance to live nearby. Open houses serve a similar purpose, creating activity and conversation around a new listing.

6. Waiting until the home is listed to plan staging and marketing

A good staging and marketing conversation should happen before the home goes live. You only get one first launch. Before listing, I want sellers thinking about what to declutter, what needs attention before the photoshoot, how to price for the current market, who the likely buyer is and how we'll get their attention.

7. Making decisions based only on what a neighbor's home sold for

Comparable sales matter, but one nearby sale doesn't determine what your home is worth. Your neighbor may have had a different floor plan, a full remodel, better presentation, or sold in different market conditions. The better question is: what are buyers likely to pay for this specific home in the current market?

8. Forgetting that buyers compare your home with everything else available

You're not selling in a vacuum. Buyers compare price, condition, location, upgrades, monthly costs, layout and presentation across several homes. A thoughtfully prepared, staged and professionally photographed home may already have an advantage before the buyer knows anything else about it.

What should Eastlake homeowners do before selling?

Start planning before you're ready to put the sign in the yard. Understand what your home may realistically be worth, decide how to prepare and stage it, then photograph it and build the marketing plan around that presentation. That plan can include strategic pricing, staging, professional photography, digital marketing, social media advertising, open houses and neighborhood outreach.

Prepare first. Launch with intention. Market from day one. My Seller's Guide walks through each step.

FAQ: Selling a home in Eastlake, Chula Vista

Should I stage my Eastlake home before professional photography?

Yes. Staging and preparing the home before the photoshoot helps create the strongest possible online first impression.

Is putting my Eastlake home on the MLS enough marketing?

I don't believe it should be the entire strategy. My approach can include digital marketing, social media ads, open houses, staging, professional photography and neighborhood outreach in addition to MLS exposure.
