Is Now a Good Time to Sell a Home in Chula Vista, CA?

By Betty Torano, REALTOR® · Eastlake resident since 1987 · August 18, 2026

If you're wondering whether now is a good time to sell your home in Chula Vista, the answer depends on more than what you're hearing about the housing market.

Buyers are still buying, but they're paying close attention to price, condition and value. Sellers can absolutely get strong results, but how you prepare, price and market your home matters. I've seen firsthand that when a home is positioned correctly, buyers respond.

Here's what I'd want you to know if you were sitting across from me asking, "Betty, should I sell my Chula Vista home now or wait?"

What's happening in the Chula Vista housing market?

There really isn't one "Chula Vista housing market." A home in Rolling Hills Ranch may attract a different buyer and face different competition than one in Eastlake, Otay Ranch, Rancho del Rey, San Miguel Ranch or another part of Chula Vista. Even two homes in the same neighborhood can have different values based on condition, location, lot, upgrades and layout.

What matters to you as a seller is what buyers are willing to pay for a home like yours, in your neighborhood, right now.

Is now a good time to sell in Chula Vista?

It can be. But I wouldn't tell you to sell simply because the market looks favorable. I'd want to know why you're thinking about selling. Are you relocating? Downsizing? Do you need more space? Have you built equity you want to use toward your next home?

There's no way to know exactly what home prices or mortgage rates will do six months or a year from now. Instead of trying to time the perfect market, I'd look at what your home could realistically sell for today, what you might walk away with, and whether selling helps you accomplish your next move. That gives you real numbers to work with.

Pricing your Chula Vista home correctly

Sellers naturally want the highest price possible, but starting with the highest listing price doesn't necessarily produce the highest selling price. Buyers can see new listings, recent sales, price reductions and how long homes have been sitting. If your home feels overpriced compared with the competition, they may not negotiate. They may simply move on.

A recent sale in Rolling Hills Ranch

I recently represented a homeowner in Rolling Hills Ranch in Chula Vista, and it's a good example of how I approach the process. We didn't just put the home on the market and hope buyers would show up. We prepared it. The home was professionally staged, photographed by a professional, strategically marketed, and drew strong traffic at the open house.

But one of the most important decisions was the price. We priced the home right to sell. We received multiple offers, and the home closed above the list price.

That doesn't mean every Chula Vista home will sell over asking. I'd never promise that. What it shows is what can happen when staging, professional photography, strategic marketing and smart pricing work together.

Should you fix up your home before selling?

You may need to do some work, but that doesn't automatically mean a major remodel. Talk with a real estate professional before spending thousands on renovations. Sometimes fresh paint, decluttering, landscaping, deep cleaning, minor repairs or staging make a meaningful difference. You're preparing the home to sell, not remodeling it for yourself.

4 mistakes I'd avoid when selling in Chula Vista

  1. Starting too high on price. Overpricing can cost you valuable attention during the first days on the market.
  2. Listing before the home is ready. The photos, staging, repairs and marketing should be ready before buyers see the listing.
  3. Spending money on the wrong improvements. Figure out which improvements buyers actually care about first.
  4. Making your decision based on national headlines. National news doesn't tell you what's happening in Rolling Hills Ranch, Eastlake, Otay Ranch or your neighborhood.

More on this in the biggest mistakes Eastlake sellers make.

Should you sell now or wait?

Start with three questions: What could your home realistically sell for today? What would you likely walk away with? Does that put you in a good position for what you want to do next? If those numbers work and you have a reason to move, selling now may make sense. If they don't, waiting could be the better decision. Sometimes the right answer is "not yet," and that's okay.

Frequently asked questions

Are homes in Chula Vista still getting multiple offers?

Some are. My recent Rolling Hills Ranch listing received multiple offers and closed above list price, but not every seller should expect that. Pricing, condition, marketing, competition and buyer demand all matter.

How do I know what my Chula Vista home is worth?
