A Complete Guide to the Eastlake HOAs and Amenities in Eastlake, Chula Vista, CA

By Betty Torano, REALTOR® · Eastlake resident since 1987 · December 10, 2025

Eastlake is organized into three homeowners associations, each serving specific neighborhoods with its own pools, clubs and parks. Here's how they break down, what each one offers, and what buyers should know about HOA dues.

I

Eastlake I

Eastlake Hills · Eastlake Shores

The original Eastlake HOA, home to many of the community's most iconic features. Lakeside views, parks and gathering spaces give it a classic Eastlake feel.

HOA amenities

  • Eastlake Beach Club and Pavilion
  • Eastlake Lagoon and sand volleyball courts
  • Eastlake Swim and Tennis Club
  • Eastlake Shorebird Pool
  • Paddle boats and the lake walking path
  • Parks and playgrounds

A great fit for: lake lovers and anyone who wants the original Eastlake charm.

II

Eastlake II

Eastlake Greens

Eastlake Greens is known for golf course views, rolling greenbelts and an active lifestyle.

HOA amenities

  • Eastlake Tennis and Swim Club, with pickleball courts
  • Eastlake Dolphin Beach
  • Augusta Park and Pool
  • Parks and playgrounds

Public amenities nearby

  • Enagic Golf Club at Eastlake
  • Sunset View Park
  • Chula Vista Community Park

A great fit for: golfers and anyone who loves tennis, pickleball and green open space.

III

Eastlake III

Eastlake Trails · Trails North · The Woods · Eastlake Vistas

HOA III covers Eastlake's easternmost neighborhoods, known for hillside views, large parks and beautiful recreation centers.

HOA amenities

  • Eastlake Creekside Clubhouse
  • Eastlake Woods Clubhouse
  • Eastlake Pond
  • Parks and playgrounds

Public amenities nearby

  • Eastlake Little League fields
  • Salt Creek Park and Soccer Arena
  • Centennial Trail
  • Mountain Hawk Park
  • Elite Athlete Training Center
  • Upper Otay Reservoir trailhead

A great fit for: hikers, nature lovers and anyone who enjoys being outdoors.

Why some homes pay two HOAs

Every Eastlake home belongs to one of the three master associations above. Some homes also belong to a smaller sub-association for their own tract, condo complex or gated neighborhood.

Master HOA

Eastlake I, II or III

  • Beach Club and clubhouses
  • Community pools and parks
  • Common landscaping

Sub-association

Only some homes

  • Private pool or spa
  • Gates and private streets
  • Building exteriors, roofs or insurance

Sub-associations are most common in condos, townhomes and gated neighborhoods. A single-family home on a public street may pay only the master HOA, while a condo in the same neighborhood may pay both.

Before you write an offer, ask for:

  1. The total monthly dues, master plus any sub-association, added together
  2. The HOA documents for each association, which California sellers must provide (Civil Code §4525)
  3. What each association covers, such as exterior maintenance or insurance

HOA dues and Mello-Roos are different

HOA dues pay your association. Mello-Roos is a special tax on your county property tax bill that helped fund Eastlake's schools and infrastructure, and it varies by neighborhood and parcel. See my Mello-Roos in Eastlake guide to learn how to check any home.
